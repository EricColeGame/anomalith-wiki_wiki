## Protecting Yourself from Social Media Scams and Clones

Due to the monetary value associated with gaming inventories, popular creator handles attract impersonators attempting phishing schemes. Keeping your inventory secure requires diligence whenever searching for an **anomalith twitter** account or interacting with giveaway posts.

Phishing bots frequently mimic avatar images, bio text, and display names while slightly altering the handle (e.g., adding an extra letter or underscore). Always inspect follower counts, account creation dates, and verified badges before interacting with any link.

| Security Checkpoint | Legitimate Creator Profile | Impersonator / Phishing Clone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Follower Count** | Hundreds of thousands of active followers | Low follower count (often < 1,000) |
| **Account Age** | Established years ago with continuous post history | Created recently (days or weeks old) |
| **Direct Messages (DMs)** | Rarely initiates private DMs claiming you won | Sends unsolicited messages with urgent claim links |
| **Link Destinations** | Official landing pages and verified partners | Suspicious URL variations and login fakeouts |

### Best Practices for Account Safety

- **Never enter Steam credentials** on unknown login portals linked directly from social comments.
- **Enable Steam Guard Mobile Authenticator** to prevent unauthorized trade confirmations.
- **Cross-reference handle spellings** against official YouTube video descriptions or Twitch stream panels.
- **Block and report** suspicious accounts immediately to protect other community members.
